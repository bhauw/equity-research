# The 8 Investor Lenses — Independent, Equal Weight

Run after the qualitative phase, as one pass. Each lens reaches its own verdict **without
reference to the others**. All 8 carry equal weight. Disagreement is expected, valuable, and
must not be reconciled. Score each 1–10 (total /80) alongside the verdict wording below.

## Warren Buffett — Quality & Moat
Criteria: durable moat, simple business, high ROIC with minimal leverage, predictable earnings,
long reinvestment runway, management integrity.
Verdict: "Likely Buffett compounder" / "Good business, insufficient moat durability" /
"Outside circle of competence"

## Bill Ackman — Concentrated High-Conviction
Criteria: high FCF generation, pricing power, clear path to operational improvement or capital
return, identifiable catalyst, downside protection from balance sheet and moat.
Verdict: "Suitable for concentrated high-conviction position" / "Quality present but lacks
catalyst" / "Insufficient asymmetry for large allocation"

## Ray Dalio — Macro & Cycle
Criteria: sensitivity to rates, inflation, FX, global liquidity; cyclical vs structural exposure;
balance-sheet resilience; geographic/geopolitical diversification; position in the current regime.
Verdict: "Well-positioned across macro regimes" / "Cycle-dependent — timing critical" /
"Macro headwinds outweigh fundamentals"

## Stanley Druckenmiller — Growth + Liquidity + Trend
Criteria: earnings growth acceleration or inflection, industry trend strength, secular tailwinds,
operating leverage to the upside, risk-reward asymmetry over 12–36 months.
Verdict: "Strong trend + growth acceleration candidate" / "Growth stable but no acceleration" /
"Insufficient momentum or asymmetry"

## David Tepper — Opportunistic & Asymmetric
Criteria: balance-sheet optionality, mispricing vs intrinsic value, downside protection in stress,
recovery or re-rating potential, skewed payoff across macro outcomes.
Verdict: "Attractive asymmetric opportunity" / "Fairly priced — limited upside skew" /
"Downside risk dominates"

## Seth Klarman — Deep Value & Margin of Safety
Criteria: discount to intrinsic value, asset coverage, downside protection as the primary filter,
avoidance of permanent capital loss over return maximization; catalysts optional.
Focus: what is the asset value, what is the downside if wrong, is there a margin of safety?
Verdict: "Compelling margin of safety — Klarman-style opportunity" / "Reasonable value but
insufficient discount" / "Price exceeds conservative asset value"

## Howard Marks — Risk-Adjusted & Cycle-Aware
Criteria: where is the market in the cycle; is consensus overly optimistic or pessimistic; is the
outcome distribution skewed up or down; is the investor adequately compensated for the risk?
Focus: second-level thinking — what does the market miss; is sentiment tailwind or headwind?
Verdict: "Risk-adjusted return is attractive — market underestimates quality" / "Fair
compensation for risk — not a standout opportunity" / "Market overestimates upside — risk underpriced"

## Carl Icahn — Activist & Turnaround
Criteria: identifiable value unlock, underperforming management, lazy balance sheet (excess cash,
under-levered), spinoff/asset sale/buyback opportunity, would activist pressure accelerate value?
Focus: the gap between current value and what an engaged owner could extract.
Verdict: "Strong activist opportunity — clear value unlock available" / "Operational improvement
possible but limited activist angle" / "Shareholder value already optimized — no activist case"
